Privacy Policy

Latch — Private Gallery · Last updated 1 October 2026

Latch is a private photo and video library that runs entirely on your device. This policy describes what the app does with your information. It is short because the answer is, in almost every case, nothing.

Summary Latch collects no personal data. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party services that receive information about you. Your photos and videos are stored only inside the app on your own device, and are never uploaded anywhere by the app.

Information we collect

None. The developer of Latch receives no information about you or your use of the app. Specifically, the app contains no analytics framework, no crash reporting service, no advertising identifier, no social login, and no networking code that contacts a server operated by the developer.

Your photos and videos

Media you import is copied into the app's own private storage area (the iOS application sandbox), which other apps cannot read. Imported files are stored byte-for-byte as received — the app does not re-encode them.

This storage is marked as excluded from iCloud and iTunes/Finder device backups. That is a deliberate privacy choice: it means your private library is not copied into a backup that may be stored elsewhere. It has an important consequence, described in the warning below.

Your library is not backed up Because the app's storage is excluded from backups, deleting Latch, erasing your device, or moving to a new device will permanently destroy everything stored in the app. There is no copy anywhere else, and the developer cannot recover it. Use the app's export features to keep copies of anything you cannot afford to lose.

Your passcode

Latch protects its library with a numeric passcode that you choose. The passcode itself is never stored. The app stores only a salted cryptographic hash of it in the iOS Keychain, and compares later entries against that hash.

There is no recovery mechanism, no reset link, and no backdoor. If you forget your passcode, neither you nor the developer can unlock the library. This is a consequence of the app having no server and no account: there is nobody to ask.

Face ID and Touch ID

If you enable biometric unlock, authentication is performed by iOS itself. The app asks the system whether authentication succeeded and receives only a yes or no. Your fingerprint and face data never reach the app, and cannot — iOS does not expose them to apps.

Optional automatic deletion

Latch offers a setting, off by default, that erases all data in the app after a number of consecutive incorrect passcode entries. It only ever takes effect if you turn it on yourself. When it does, the deletion is immediate and permanent.

System permissions the app asks for

Photo library
Read access is used only to copy the specific items you select into the app. The app does not browse or index your photo library on its own. Write access is requested only at the moment you explicitly export an item back to the Photos app.
Local network
Used only by the optional web server feature described below. The app does not scan your network or discover other devices.
Face ID
Used only to unlock the app, and only if you enable it.

If you decline any of these, the rest of the app continues to work; only the feature that needs the permission is unavailable.

The local web server

Latch includes an optional server that lets you browse and transfer your library from a computer on the same Wi-Fi network. When you turn it on:

  • It listens only on your local network. It is not reachable from the internet, and the app does not configure port forwarding or any relay.
  • It requires a username and password that the app shows you, and it limits repeated failed sign-in attempts.
  • It runs only while Latch is open on screen. Switching apps or locking the device stops it.
  • Nothing is sent to the developer or to any third party. The only parties involved are your device and the computer you connect from.

Traffic on your local network is not encrypted in transit. On a home or personal network this is comparable to any other device-to-device transfer, but you should not enable the server on a public or untrusted Wi-Fi network.

Purchases

Latch offers a one-time in-app purchase that unlocks additional features. The purchase is processed entirely by Apple. The developer never sees your payment details, your Apple Account, or your name. The app checks whether a purchase exists using Apple's on-device receipt verification; no purchase information is sent to the developer or to any other server.

Children

Latch is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, including children.

Third-party code

The app includes the open-source FlyingFox HTTP server library (MIT licence), which runs locally on your device as part of the web server feature. It is a software component, not a service: it sends no data anywhere and its authors receive nothing about you.

Changes to this policy
